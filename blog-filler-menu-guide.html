Building a filler menu at your spa: where to start

October 8, 2026 · Miracle Healthcare editorial team

A new aesthetic menu does not need fifteen filler SKUs. It needs the right three, stocked reliably, with a clear story for each. Here is the structure we see working for clinics and licensed spas building their first injectable menu.

First, the one rule of filler menus

Fillers are hyaluronic acid (HA) gels, and the single most important property is firmness. Soft, low-viscosity gels belong in superficial layers — fine lines, delicate areas. Firm, cohesive gels belong deeper — folds, volume, contour. A menu that respects this split sells itself, because every customer concern maps cleanly to one product.

The starter trio

1. A soft gel for fine work — NEURAMIS Light

Low-viscosity, made with Medytox's two-step SHAPE cross-linking for low residual BDDE. This covers fine periorbital and perioral lines and subtle lip definition — the entry-level treatments that introduce hesitant customers to injectables.

2. A firm workhorse with lidocaine — NEURAMIS Deep Lidocaine

The volume and fold product: nasolabial folds, marionette lines, chin and mid-face support. The integrated 0.3% lidocaine matters commercially as much as clinically — comfortable first treatments create second treatments.

3. A value tier without a second brand — NEURAMIS Deep (no lidocaine)

The lidocaine-free NEURAMIS Deep sits at the same sharp price point and lets you run promotional pricing without touching your premium line's positioning — same manufacturer, simpler stock.

What to add later

Once demand is proven: a volumizing gel for cheek and jawline work, a home-care tier (NEURADERM Core Time as the entry point), and consumables at scale — topical anesthetic and fine-gauge syringes move with every session.

The boring essentials

  • Fillers are professional products: menus, consent forms and the treating practitioner's license need to be in order before the first syringe is opened.
  • Buy from a supplier who shows lot numbers and expiry on every box and can produce import documentation.
  • Track cost per treatment, not cost per syringe — a cheaper gel that needs more volume is not cheaper.
