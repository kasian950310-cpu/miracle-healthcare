INNOTOX vs Botulax: how Korean clinics choose their toxin

October 2, 2026 · Miracle Healthcare editorial team

Walk into ten aesthetic clinics in Seoul and you will find both of these names in the refrigerator. INNOTOX by Medytox and Botulax by Hugel are two of the most widely used botulinum toxins in Korea — but they sit in the fridge for different reasons. Here is how practitioners actually choose between them.

The core difference: liquid vs freeze-dried

Botulax follows the classic format: a freeze-dried (lyophilized) powder that the practitioner reconstitutes with sterile 0.9% saline before each session. It is a routine every injector knows, but it introduces two variables — dilution ratio and mixing technique — that can shift the effective concentration from vial to vial.

INNOTOX arrives as a ready-to-use liquid, pre-diluted at the factory. There is nothing to mix: the concentration is identical in every vial, every time. For clinics that run many toxin sessions per day, that removes both preparation time and a source of dosing variation.

How clinics actually decide

  • High treatment volume, price-sensitive customers: Botulax. The per-vial saving compounds across hundreds of sessions a month, and its long real-world track record in Korean clinics makes it an easy default.
  • Premium positioning or precision work: INNOTOX. The liquid format reads as a quality signal to patients, and the fixed concentration suits delicate areas where small dose differences show.
  • Returning long-term patients: many clinics also stock CORETOX, Medytox's animal-protein-free and albumin-free formulation, designed with repeated treatment cycles in mind.

Duration is broadly comparable across quality Korean type-A toxins — commonly several months, varying by patient, area and dose — so the choice usually comes down to workflow, positioning and price rather than the result itself.
